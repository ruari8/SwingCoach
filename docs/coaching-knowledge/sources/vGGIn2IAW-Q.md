## Evidence trail

### E01: [00:45 to 01:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vGGIn2IAW-Q&t=45s)

Ruffels arranges three groups of four balls: first aid, second aid and no aid. He says groups prevent mindlessly scraping balls and keep intention clear. Source card supports organizing balls into blocks.

Limit: Counts describe this practice structure, not validated universal dose.

### E02: [01:34 to 02:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vGGIn2IAW-Q&t=94s)

His stated tendencies are insufficient upper-body turn, pulling down on shaft for power and a steepening downswing. A ball between arms encourages torso turn with elbows together. From top he feels squeezing the ball makes club shallower. Source card explicitly links his underturn/steepness to this aid.

Limit: No measured shaft-plane or torso-angle change. Squeezing is his feel, not a universal cause of shallowing.

### E03: [02:26 to 03:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vGGIn2IAW-Q&t=146s)

Four ball-aid shots are presented. Ruffels warns that filming an aid swing can lead him to judge its odd appearance; it physically constrains motion, so he uses it to learn a feel and checks appearance after removing it. Slightly laid-off/inside drill positions are tolerated for this purpose.

Limit: Edited shots/rehearsals are not independently counted as a complete trial log. Tolerating a drill exaggeration does not mean accepting the same shape unconditionally in play.

### E04: [03:35 to 04:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vGGIn2IAW-Q&t=215s)

Purple band under arms is used to promote depth: Ruffels wants hands feeling behind right shoulder rather than right elbow high/in front. Losing band in that phase provides feedback. Source card describes depth as a reason for the band.

Limit: No calibrated hand-depth measure or ideal behind-shoulder target for all players.

### E05: [04:18 to 05:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vGGIn2IAW-Q&t=258s)

Second band purpose is lead-arm/chest connection when he rips through at high speed. He wants to retain band through impact or a little beyond, then release it for normal finish. Video shows band leaving and lying behind him. He warns to allow space behind because it is thrown. He reports good feel and more depth after this block.

Limit: Band release after impact is intended; do not label any release a fault. No measured face stabilization, pressure or causal test.

### E06: [05:28 to 06:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vGGIn2IAW-Q&t=328s)

Ruffels says he can get stuck in drills, whether they feel good or bad, and become too technical in tournaments. Final four balls remove aids, practice swings and his technical rehearsal waggle. He chooses a specific shot and commits attention to target, distinguishing a useful sensation from mechanical swing thoughts.

Limit: No-waggle is this transfer constraint, not a ban on natural movement or a universal routine. Improved tournament performance is untested.

### E07: [06:33 to 08:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vGGIn2IAW-Q&t=393s)

Four target-only pitching-wedge shots: hold-off fade target about 140 yards, reported 139; low draw toward back-left target, reported solid but outside Quad capture range so no numbers; stock straight target146, reported144 and straight; lower cut target129, reported126 and did not quite cut. He says this block takes longer because each shot needs deliberate selection and a matching sensation.

Limit: Distances are verbally reported from Quad context, not displayed raw readouts. No number invented for missed capture. Final shot fails intended curve despite close distance. No baseline/control or measured dispersion.

### E08: [08:12 to 09:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vGGIn2IAW-Q&t=492s)

He suggests repeating 12-shot structure across selected clubs, naming pitching wedge, eight, six and five as examples, and taking a couple-minute break after each focused set. Fewer balls can fit a time limit while keeping the break. Recap is turn, enough arm depth and squeezing elbows to shallow, all explicitly his current priorities.

Limit: Four-to-six clubs at 12 shots means48–72 for one pass; source also mentions some100-shot days without specifying structure. No optimal workload or recovery test.

## Interpretation and outcome limits

The no-aid shots target140, an unspecified low-draw distance,146 and129 yards. Reported outcomes are139, no captured reading,144 and126. The final shot did not achieve its intended cut. These are spoken results in Quad context, not inspected raw readouts. The three-block routine and breaks are practice suggestions; they do not establish an optimal dose or tournament improvement.
