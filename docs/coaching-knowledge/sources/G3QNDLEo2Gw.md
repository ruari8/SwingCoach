## Why this source matters

The coach changes the assignment when the evidence changes. Here he diagnoses an upper-body lean followed by a stalled pivot and arm throw. He first stabilises the backswing, then introduces transition work. In the first source, he had explicitly withheld extra transition instruction because he judged that part adequate.

The source also makes task differences explicit. The full-swing exercise uses a hip-led pivot; the basic pitch uses what Chris calls a finesse sequence with arms leading before the body carries them through. A knowledge system matching only the word `rotation` would lose this distinction.

## Conditions and distinctions

1. First source versus this source: an opening feel for a closed-face tendency cannot be copied into this lesson's face check. Grip and observed face condition must travel with the cue.
2. First source versus this source: a previously adequate transition can become a current coaching finding. Preserve the observed reason for changing priorities rather than treating prior advice as permanent.
3. Full swing versus pitching: shared language about turning does not make the sequencing instructions interchangeable.
4. Pitch result versus contact: Chris praises a high, soft result but says it is not the assigned shot. Store intended landing/trajectory separately from strike quality.
5. Bunker terminology: collecting sand on the club in a rehearsal and scooping the wrists through a ball shot are different actions.
