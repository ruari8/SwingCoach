## What changes the coaching decision

Menante thinks extreme lag costs him speed. Rose initially suspects it is helping him compensate for limited body power. Force and 3D review then distinguish good power generation from a driver sequence in which arms peak before trunk. That pattern often suggests casting to Rose, but the visible extreme lag contradicts that explanation. Player intent and further review lead him to early arm drop with a late trunk.

The selected work is trunk rotation relative to pelvis plus longer-term upper/core power development. Lag is retained as useful. Driver usually misses right; irons miss left/two-way. Those conditions stay separate.

Kneeling driver shots look better to the coach, but the first standing transfer fails. The initial lower-ab feel disappears, a kneeling cut produces a different shoulder-turn feel, and later standing replay shows only partial improvement. That sequence is useful coaching knowledge in its own right.

## Evidence trail

### E01: [00:30–10:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcG-r_13z_I&t=30s)

Menante describes his father as his coach, self-directed training aided by ChatGPT, a wish for more speed, recent lower-back trouble and a belief that excessive lag is a fault. Rose asks whether reducing lag has ever actually improved performance; no demonstrated improvement is established. Later dialogue clarifies driver usually misses right, irons left/two-way.

Limit: Initial broad left-miss wording is refined by club later. Back history is reported context, not a diagnosis.

### E02: [10:10–17:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcG-r_13z_I&t=610s)

Physical screening distinguishes an already arched starting posture from inability to tilt farther forward. Left trunk rotation feels harder but is reported to travel farther than right. Wrist mobility is not unusually large despite visible lag; Rose asks about grip retention rather than concluding he lets go. Left loaded hip and right ankle findings are minor context, not the main power explanation.

Limit: No formal diagnostic report, exact trunk angles or causal physical-to-swing test. Feeling resistance is not itself proof of reduced range.

### E03: [17:10–21:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcG-r_13z_I&t=1030s)

Strength checks use estimated ability to complete eight repetitions, including 55 dumbbells and estimated cable values 42 left/47 right. Jump and seated throws follow. Rose judges lower-body power relatively good but upper-body/core power low despite reasonable strength, and proposes a tested, targeted programme with a trainer.

Limit: Do not label estimated repetitions as measured eight-repetition maxima. Caption 174 is ambiguous, and grip 42/44 has no verified units. No raw test report.

### E04: [21:50–29:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcG-r_13z_I&t=1310s)

Instrumented swings and replay establish an extreme lag appearance. Player mentions outside takeaway/trail-arm position and dislikes the driver image. Driver miss is clarified as usually right and iron miss as left/two-way. The player has not established a better-performing reduced-lag swing.

Limit: Source explanation; not independently validated biomechanics.

### E05: [29:25–31:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcG-r_13z_I&t=1765s)

Rose states a hypothesis before data review: lag/release may compensate for limited body power. He distinguishes hip-to-shoulder, trunk-to-arm and arm-to-club contributions and wants another source of power before removing the existing contribution.

Limit: Mechanical analogies are coaching explanations, not measured energy partition.

### E06: [32:30–34:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcG-r_13z_I&t=1950s)

Limit: Spoken 229 differs from displayed 216. Trial identity is unavailable; do not silently reconcile or assume matched shots. Other channels do not necessarily share units.

### E07: [34:00–37:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcG-r_13z_I&t=2040s)

Kinematic review reports club angular velocity 2537 degrees per second. Cropped graphic shows pelvis/trunk/lead-arm order 1/3/2 and speeds 506/709/1079. Rose identifies arms peaking before trunk with driver; iron order is better despite its larger directional problem.

Limit: Club columns are cropped in these graphic frames. Club 2537 retained as spoken reading. Peak order alone does not identify a cause.

### E08: [37:35–42:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcG-r_13z_I&t=2255s)

Rose first associates this sequence with casting but rejects that usual explanation because Menante shows extreme lag. Player reports keeping his chest still to cover the ball. Kneeling rehearsal is difficult. Rose instead explains early arm drop with a late trunk, and wants trunk rotation relative to pelvis. Separation is described as 61 degrees at the top and 20 at impact.

Limit: Separation values are spoken and no raw angular definitions are provided. Do not adopt zero separation as a universal target.

### E09: [42:00–46:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcG-r_13z_I&t=2520s)

Rose recommends improving trunk/upper-body power with a trainer, keeping lag as a useful contribution, and testing trunk rotation before direct hand changes. He predicts longer-term improvement and warns that removing lag now could lose speed. He connects a more open trunk to reducing this player's iron left miss.

Limit: Future power/speed gains and back-health hopes are not observed results. The face/path explanation is source-specific and cannot guarantee elimination of left misses.

### E10: [46:15–48:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcG-r_13z_I&t=2775s)

Kneeling driver shots use a teed ball and both knees on a towel. Rose explains that the constraint encourages trunk rotation relative to the lower body while still using ground support. Replay is judged to show less extreme lag and earlier release without asking the player to cast. Rose reports reduced left exit tendency.

Limit: No matched post-drill launch or 3D numbers. Sampled address, finish and replay do not measure continuous sequencing.

### E11: [48:00–51:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcG-r_13z_I&t=2880s)

Rose describes a two-step coaching process: first demonstrate that a drill changes its intended target using video or measurement, then find the player's own repeatable feel and transfer it to standing. Menante initially feels lower abs, cannot reproduce it standing, returns to kneeling and reports the sensation fading.

Limit: Source explanation; not independently validated biomechanics.

### E12: [51:00–54:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcG-r_13z_I&t=3060s)

An arm cue and a left-leg-forward rehearsal do not resolve the transfer. Returning to kneeling and attempting a cut produces a repeatable shoulders-pulled-left feel. Rose then tries combining that with right-arm straightening. He explains exaggerated opposite-direction practice as a feel, not a normal-swing target.

Limit: Caption wording alternates arm collapsing/straightening. Preserve trial chronology; only the explicitly combined later cues form the two-cue option.

### E13: [54:00–55:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcG-r_13z_I&t=3240s)

Standing replay shows less extreme lag early in the downswing but persistent late hold near impact. Coach explicitly says the kneeling drill helped the bottom whereas standing has mainly helped the top. He recommends a row of seven to ten teed kneeling shots, moving along the row, then standing with the same trunk-turn feel.

Limit: Seven to ten repetitions are the source prescription, not proof of required dosage. No retention test.

### E14: [55:35–57:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcG-r_13z_I&t=3335s)

Rose revisits the speed tradeoff. Six-to-twelve-month gains and doubling power in two years are goals/expectations; he cautions that the swing change could initially cost a mile or two per hour. Menante accepts a longer-term approach.

Limit: No longitudinal measurement or proven future speed increase.

### E15: [57:35–60:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcG-r_13z_I&t=3455s)

Iron trials test turning the trunk left while trying a draw/hook and then a small cut. Rose judges the left miss reduced even with a hook intent. Player says the cut requires feeling the club more in front. These are player/coach reports with visible attempts, not quantified dispersion.

Limit: Source explanation; not independently validated biomechanics.

### E16: [60:30–63:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcG-r_13z_I&t=3630s)

Rose stresses trunk rotation relative to pelvis: rotating both together does not change their separation. He asks for the right foot about one and a half inches back to slow the lower body and let trunk rotation catch up. Player already uses this stance to get arms in front; Rose changes the intent to trunk-led positioning. Later clarification identifies early arm drop, not lag alone.

Limit: Foot offset is spoken; no calibrated image measurement. Claimed pelvic slowing is not measured in the outdoor trial.

### E17: [63:15–64:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcG-r_13z_I&t=3795s)

Coach consolidates kneeling-to-standing practice. Menante attempts a large hook while turning the trunk; Rose praises the limited left result. They then deliberately return to the old trunk-still pattern and the left miss returns according to dialogue.

Limit: Edited demonstration with no ball-flight export. Deliberate contrast does not establish retained or universal control.

### E18: [64:55–67:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcG-r_13z_I&t=3895s)

First overhead medicine-ball slam shows chest and arms moving down together according to Rose. He points out rebound-to-face risk with a different ball. No-ball rehearsal teaches squatting as arms rise, then legs driving upward as arms deliver down; start tall with ball low.

Limit: Retain the source equipment/rebound condition. No exact ball mass, load, programme dose or force measurement.

### E19: [67:00–68:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcG-r_13z_I&t=4020s)

Slam coaching asks for push from balls of feet rather than toes and a small backward jump. Player reports right-side dominance; Rose asks for a doubled left-side feel and corrects the expected rotation direction.

Limit: Two-times-left is a feel, not a measured force ratio or a general prescription.

### E20: [68:00–70:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcG-r_13z_I&t=4080s)

Half-kneeling medicine-ball bounce uses left knee down and right foot forward. Ball starts over the shoulder and is bounced toward/over the coach. Initial lower-body wobble leads to a stable-base correction; aim is trunk rotation left around pelvis with arms delivered in front.

Limit: No measured trunk speed improvement; stable-base assessment is coach observation. No general exercise clearance from this demonstration.

### E21: [70:00–73:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcG-r_13z_I&t=4200s)

Training interview finds little deliberate trunk-rotation work. Cable press in split stance pairs one arm pushing with the other elbow pulling back, braced/tucked pelvis and trunk rotation. Opposite side is demonstrated with left arm/left foot forward. Rose wants workouts aligned with the swing change.

Limit: No exact loads, sets or progression. The source's strong anti-bench wording is a priority for this player, not a general prohibition on bench training.

### E22: [73:15–76:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BcG-r_13z_I&t=4395s)

Rose asks Menante to explain the plan back to his father. Player identifies trunk rotation and power, then says pelvis slow/stopping; Rose corrects the explanation to arms firing early and interrupting trunk action. Team coordination and future speed/back-health hopes close the lesson.

Limit: Closing back-health and speed expectations are not medical or longitudinal outcomes.
