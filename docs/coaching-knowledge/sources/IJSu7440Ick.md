## What changes the decision

Allen asks for consistent contact, an explanation of his sequencing, and a way to recover without searching. The first useful change is setup: he repeatedly aims right and places stock shots too far back. Forward-spot alignment works better than a tested alternate walk-in method, but even the spot itself sometimes needs correcting.

Rose initially suspects early extension. Captured 3D later does not support that diagnosis for those swings. Video also separates acceptable early transition from an in-to-out impact-zone pattern. Good force and body-segment sequence are preserved; trunk position and pelvic sway become the main movement focus.

The electric-fence reach changes the instrumented trial and feels different, but it does not fully transfer. Continued sway leads to manual resistance, trail-foot flare and then starting around 60 right/40 left. The downswing still goes left. Some shots remain poor and execution is described as roughly one in three, so the record preserves partial success.

## Measurements and uncertainty

- Four-channel force graphic at 40:55 shows approximate normalized maxima 24.2/18.9/19.4/146.6. Later speech says lateral 24 and vertical 138. Those differences remain unresolved; the record does not assume identical trials or units across torque and force channels.
- Native thorax plots are labelled `JA-4iron-2-7` and `JA-4iron-4-reachoverthefence`. Their visible cursor readouts are at 0.529 and 1.171 seconds respectively. They are not matched checkpoints and must not be subtracted to claim improvement.
- “50% better” release and “one out of three” execution are informal coaching estimates. Sixty/forty is a setup feel, not a measured pressure split.
- The source gives daily exercise durations, but supplies no measured long-term mobility, retention, handicap or on-course outcome.

## Application limits

Physical-screen findings are context for this assessment. They do not establish a clinical diagnosis from phone footage. The source’s force, plane, fascia and eye-dominance explanations remain attributed claims, with uncertainty retained. Dramatic exercise language about dislocation is not literal instruction.

The useful selection pattern is to check setup, investigate the observed movement, test one drill, inspect the response, and adapt when necessary. In this lesson the direct release feel is tried only after setup/trunk changes partly work. A flippy label alone would miss that ordering.
