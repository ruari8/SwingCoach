# Ryan Ruffels: short-game choices by lie and target

Twelve conditional tutorial cases linked to 14 evidence spans. Full automatic transcript read and all 14 contact sheets inspected, including technique cards. No assessed student or measured improvement.

## What this source adds

The same short-game label covers incompatible instructions. Ruffels chooses quiet hands, lazy hands, firmer wrists or a steeper bounce strike according to the ball’s lie and intended flight. His three rough examples are especially useful for matching: perched on top, halfway down and buried at the base require different treatment.

The record preserves his preferences and imperfect examples. The into-grain chop is an option he dislikes. An advanced high shot does not come out as intended, a medium-rough shot still comes out dead, and the buried example happens to be best despite being the least predictable lie.

## Evidence trail

### E01: [00:28 to 02:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Radv4RcktNs&t=28s)

Basic shot from below green level with plenty of green. A tee in grip butt should keep pointing toward belt buckle while chest turns, avoiding exaggerated handle drag or wrist flick. Stand close with handle high and small shoulder motion. Source card repeats butt at buckle, raised handle and minimal wrist break. He presents this as a simple default, including under nerves.

Limit: Tee pointing at buckle is a feel/reference, not measured invariant. Claimed 80–85 percent applicability is an estimate, not a validated selection rule.

### E02: [02:50 to 03:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Radv4RcktNs&t=170s)

With less green available, Ruffels first reads the lie, contrasting sparse sandy ground with a better sitting ball. For a simple higher shot he stands farther away, lowers handle and opens face while keeping a similar quiet-hand motion. He prefers this option when nervous or less comfortable with the lie. Source card matches these setup changes.

Limit: No measured height/spin comparison or guarantee the open-face shot suits every poor lie. Start with the actual lie and required carry.

### E03: [03:58 to 05:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Radv4RcktNs&t=238s)

Advanced higher option needs a good lie. Glove logo turns more toward sky as face slides open; tee/handle returns neutral by impact, then releases. Ruffels admits first demonstration did not come out as intended. His personal feel is a heavy clubhead falling first from top rather than pulling handle. He returns to recommending quieter hands for most people; a later shot he calls fat still works acceptably.

Limit: Card says release after impact while spoken clubhead-first is a transition feel. Preserve both rather than turn them into measured release timing. Imperfect attempts are not claimed as consistent success.

### E04: [05:27 to 06:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Radv4RcktNs&t=327s)

Into-grain Bermuda example requires committing to one shot because early ground contact is less forgiving. He demonstrates a back-in-stance chop as an available method used by others, but explicitly dislikes it personally because it is low-only and contact-sensitive.

Limit: Grass/grain identified by source, not independent botanical analysis. This is a recorded alternative, not his preferred recommendation.

### E05: [06:17 to 07:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Radv4RcktNs&t=377s)

For softness into grain, his preferred lazy-hands option uses extremely light grip, described as about one, with clubhead passing hands and a shallow motion. He wants to take speed out of the flight. Source card confirms very soft grip and lazy hands.

Limit: Grip scale is subjective, not measured force. Almost falling out is an exaggerated feel, not instruction to lose the club.

### E06: [07:04 to 07:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Radv4RcktNs&t=424s)

If anxiety prevents a light hold, Ruffels removes little finger of gloved lead hand to make a nine-finger grip, intended to allow club to pass hands more easily. He demonstrates and reports soft flight, then reiterates this is his preferred method.

Limit: Optional constraint for this specific shot, not a general full-swing grip change. Contact forces and actual pressure are unmeasured.

### E07: [07:43 to 08:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Radv4RcktNs&t=463s)

Alternative into-grain/bad-lie shot raises handle strongly, puts toe down and uses quiet hands with a long shallow brushing region. Ball is allowed to chase. Source card reads high handle/toe down, dead hands and shallow. Ruffels extends potential use to hardpan, sandier or dead lies.

Limit: No measured low-point length or guarantee all hardpan/sandy lies suit this. Compare required carry/run with actual surface.

### E08: [08:35 to 10:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Radv4RcktNs&t=515s)

For back pin with room to run over a ridge, pitching wedge replaces a more lofted wedge. Same tee-at-buckle motion; do not add extra handle lean just because it is a bump-and-run. Less club loft supplies rollout. He suggests about 70/30 lead-side weight feel, demonstrates and says he should practice/use it more. A sandy example follows.

Limit: 70/30 is a feel, not force-plate result. No calibrated proximity, rollout or club bounce specification. Claim that pitching wedges have much bounce is not a universal equipment fact.

### E09: [10:50 to 11:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Radv4RcktNs&t=650s)

First rough lie is perched on top. It looks favorable but a steep entry may strike high on face and come out dead. Ruffels uses a wide shallow arc, feeling the club brushes across the top of grass rather than cutting underneath; intended strike moves toward face center. Card repeats wide and shallow.

Limit: Source identifies perched height; sampled wide frames cannot quantify ball depth. No measured impact mark or confirmed face-center contact. Bottoming out back is an exaggerated feel here, not a fat-shot instruction for every lie.

### E10: [11:47 to 12:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Radv4RcktNs&t=707s)

Second rough lie sits halfway down. Grass before ball is expected, so he chooses firmer wrists/grip around seven or eight out of ten and enough speed to feel as though hitting seven/eight yards beyond hole. Source card says firm wrists, 8/10 grip and aim past hole. Even the demonstration comes out somewhat dead by his report.

Limit: Grip scale and extra yards are player sensations, not measured pressure or reliable distance correction. Actual speed loss depends on lie.

### E11: [12:30 to 13:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Radv4RcktNs&t=750s)

Third rough lie is buried near ground with much grass behind ball. Quiet-hands shallow motion is rejected for this lie. Ruffels picks club up more, opens face and uses speed to slap the rear/toe bounce area into ground while avoiding leading-edge dig. He calls it unpredictable even for a skilled player; this demonstration happens to be best of the three.

Limit: No measured rough depth, entry angle or landing proximity. Best demonstration does not make this the most predictable option; source explicitly says the opposite.

### E12: [14:00 to 15:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Radv4RcktNs&t=840s)

Deep bunker and short-sided target require height. Ruffels uses wide stance, opens face and moves handle back until face points toward target. Trail hand sits on top in a weak orientation, intended to resist face closing and help throw clubhead with speed. Source card preserves these four choices. Several shots are shown; one is called unlucky.

Limit: Weak refers to hand orientation, not the one-out-of-ten pressure of lazy hands. No measured face/loft, sand firmness/depth, entry point or landing distance. Not every bunker lie supports this shot.

### E13: [15:44 to 16:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Radv4RcktNs&t=944s)

Ruffels clarifies that a face aimed at target does not mean square stance. He prefers a closed stance because it helps his chest stay down/in the shot; open stance can make him push toward target or help ball upward. Card says closed stance, keeps chest down.

Limit: Personal tendency and feel, not universal stance geometry. No calibrated alignment or evidence all open stances cause lifting.

### E14: [16:22 to 16:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Radv4RcktNs&t=982s)

To reach a farther bunker pin with more run, he squares face somewhat, narrows stance and stands taller, keeping handle neutral rather than ahead. Demonstration aims for lower flight and more rollout. Closing high-launch success cue belongs to bunker learning context and is not a numeric pass/fail rule.

Limit: No measured flight/rollout comparison. More square face is relative to preceding open-face setup; high launch alone does not establish correct sand contact or distance control.

## Conditions that must survive retrieval

A one-out-of-ten soft-grip feel belongs to the selected shallow into-grain shot. Seven or eight out of ten belongs to medium rough resisting the club. Weak trail-hand grip in the bunker describes orientation, not pressure. These instructions should never be combined into an average grip rule.

The pitching-wedge runner uses lower club loft to create rollout without extra handle drag. The high short bunker shot uses an open face and handle back. A farther bunker target changes face, stance width and posture. A distant swing video may not show enough lie detail to choose between these cases.

Grip scales, 70/30 weight and seven/eight extra yards are subjective feels. There are no force, spin, launch or calibrated proximity values. The source’s tee-at-buckle reference is a teaching aid, not a measured constant through every frame.
