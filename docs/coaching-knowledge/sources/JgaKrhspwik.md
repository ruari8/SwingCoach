## What this source adds

Harrington checks grip function through lifting the club and allowing arm extension, while accepting variation in knuckle count and grip style. A secure hold should still allow wrist movement. He shows how forming the grip before grounding the club can avoid the high-hand position he deliberately demonstrates.

His setup sequence uses small movements to settle natural knee flex and hanging arms. The head remains free to move with the turn. Neither locked-down head nor deliberately elevated chin is his instruction.

## What should not become a diagnostic rule

Two and a half knuckles is a preference; two or three waggles is a routine suggestion. Neither is a validated threshold. Grip security is distinct from strong hand orientation, and relaxed wrists are compatible with hands staying seated.

The two demonstration shots have no measured ball-flight or improvement data. The opening teaser duplicates later footage. The rough/consecutive-ball practice sentence contains an unresolved automatic-caption negation, so it is retained as uncertain source material rather than offered as a confirmed drill.
