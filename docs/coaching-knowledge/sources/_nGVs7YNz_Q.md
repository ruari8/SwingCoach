# Ryan Ruffels: driver speed and effort selection

[Drive it Further With These Easy Steps…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_nGVs7YNz_Q) | Ryan Ruffels | Published 20240822 | 09:50

6 cases linked to 11 evidence spans. Full transcript read; visual review method and limits recorded below. Source checked, not independently coach-validated.

Ruffels builds speed through turn, length and softer hands, then adds an arm/chest control feel. He separates the hardest effort he would use on a wide-open hole from maximum-speed practice, where he accepts less strike, direction and spin control. The recorded sequence includes a ball-speed decrease at the connection step.

## Evidence trail

### E01: [00:52 to 01:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_nGVs7YNz_Q&t=52s)

Ruffels wants a longer speed swing created through torso and hip turn rather than arms dragging independently. Hands, arms and body start takeaway together. Longer backswing is presented as more time/stretch. Source cards support body turn and length.

Limit: No measured hip/torso angle, stretch or segment timing. More turn/length is a speed option, not a universal cure.

### E02: [01:42 to 02:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_nGVs7YNz_Q&t=102s)

He contrasts tensing forearms and gripping hard when trying for speed with a very soft loose hold. Almost letting club fall out is his exaggerated feel for allowing lag; later he clarifies hands support club and do not death-grip it.

Limit: No grip-force measure or proof more lag always means more speed. Maintain control of club; do not prescribe literal loss of grip.

### E03: [02:18 to 02:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_nGVs7YNz_Q&t=138s)

Ruffels describes his own high-effort tendency: lead arm moves off chest and hands travel far out, with unstable face by his account. He wants lead-upper-arm/chest connection while rotating.

Limit: Personal tendency rather than diagnosis of all golfers. Later he explicitly says contact is not maintained completely.

### E04: [02:47 to 03:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_nGVs7YNz_Q&t=167s)

First fuller-turn shot is demonstrated, with source circles around torso/pelvis. Result card displays 178 ball speed and 299 carry; he says he is still warming up.

Limit: Source-added circles illustrate movement, not measured rotation. Card is edited source report, not a raw launch-monitor export. Warm-up and effort vary across shots.

### E05: [03:25 to 04:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_nGVs7YNz_Q&t=205s)

Longer than his fairway-finder/normal swing, Ruffels wants driver shaft at least parallel at top, while normally slightly short. Source horizontal/shaft lines illustrate that reference. Card shows 181 ball speed and 315 carry, resolving missing digit in automatic caption.

Limit: Parallel is this player's speed-swing target, not universal threshold. No calibrated shaft angle. Caption 35 is not used as carry.

### E06: [04:02 to 04:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_nGVs7YNz_Q&t=242s)

He adds softer hands while retaining turn and length, using small steps/waggles to avoid tension. Card shows 184 ball speed and 322 carry.

Limit: One cumulative example cannot isolate grip-pressure effect. No measured force or lag change.

### E07: [04:51 to 05:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_nGVs7YNz_Q&t=291s)

For control at higher effort, he repeats upper-arm/chest connection. He clarifies it will not stay entirely touching; intention brings hand path inward, club outward and stabilizes face by his account. The combined shot card shows 183 ball speed and 323 carry.

Limit: Ball speed decreases from previous 184 despite general faster-through-each-step commentary. No measured face stability or hand-path radius. Do not pin arm rigidly to chest.

### E08: [05:46 to 06:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_nGVs7YNz_Q&t=346s)

Ruffels describes repeated speed work and pushing familiar effort limits over time. He suggests 20–30 hard shots a day as his example and rejects expecting one session to create durable 170-to-180 ball-speed improvement. He distinguishes already-practiced feels from a beginner thinking of all steps.

Limit: Dose is source suggestion, not personalized training program; no recovery, readiness, adherence or longitudinal data. Mental-limit explanation is not independently validated.

### E09: [06:36 to 07:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_nGVs7YNz_Q&t=396s)

Two attempts at the fastest effort he would use on a wide-open course hole are shown. Stable result cards show 186/334 and 186/336. He distinguishes this from a still faster effort where he loses control.

Limit: These are range examples of claimed on-course effort, not actual course outcomes. Transient rolling digits at 448 seconds are graphic animation, not a separate carry result. No dispersion statistic.

## Interpretation and outcome limits

Eight shot results are preserved. Seven have inspected stable source cards; 189/341 is a spoken result without a verified stable card. Ball-speed units are not printed on these cards; carry is described in yards. Club-speed ranges are explicitly estimates. Warm-up, accumulated cues and changing effort prevent assigning each result change to one intervention. The source’s practice dose is not a personalized daily program.

## Relationship to earlier sources

Brennan’s optional shorter-turn work contrasts with Ruffels’ longer speed swing. Neither establishes one ideal backswing length.

Harrington wants hands securely seated with free wrists; Ruffels clarifies soft hands still support the club. Do not equate softness with slipping.

Menante’s measured lag/timing pattern differs from Ruffels’ general soft-grip explanation. More lag must not become an automatic target.

Allen’s guarded intent and Ruffels’ selected course-fast effort both show that intended effort matters; the acceptable dispersion still needs player evidence.
