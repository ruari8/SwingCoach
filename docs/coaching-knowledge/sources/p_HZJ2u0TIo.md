## What this source adds

Harrington explains useful distinctions that a keyword-based library would lose. A supported trail foot in backswing differs from inward rolling practice in downswing. Trail-knee straightening is allowed but not required; forcing lead-knee straightening is discouraged. Hips should lead with a connected pull on torso, rather than spin independently.

He starts by telling golfers with effective hip movement not to change it because of the tutorial. The cases retain this exclusion. They also distinguish deliberate bad examples, slow rehearsals and seven-iron demonstrations. One shot is called thin, so the demonstrations are not represented as a flawless before/after result.

### E04: [02:45–04:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p_HZJ2u0TIo&t=165s)

A small initial bump is allowed but not required. After that, Harrington demonstrates turning into the right hip crease, using fingers at the front crease to feel it close. Trail foot remains planted rather than rolling outward, and pelvis moves back behind rather than drifting outward.

Limit: Pinch describes skin/crease closing under fingers, not a request to create joint pain. Internal rotation and pressure are source descriptions, not measured joint angles or force.

### E05: [04:08–05:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p_HZJ2u0TIo&t=248s)

Harrington prefers relaxed trail knee over ankle in the down-the-line view rather than deliberately maintaining excessive bend. He allows straightening if overall function remains good but prefers some flex. Trail hip rises relative to lead hip as pelvis tilts in backswing.

Limit: No calibrated knee or pelvis angles. Straight trail leg is allowed conditionally, not required or recommended for every golfer.

### E06: [05:40–06:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p_HZJ2u0TIo&t=340s)

Source proposes that too-level hips in backswing can make a player raise/thrust pelvis while trying to shift left. Higher trail hip is presented as making the lateral transition easier. He rehearses a leftward bump while back initially remains to target.

Limit: This is Harrington's proposed mechanism, not a universal cause of early extension. Brief transition cue does not mean holding torso back throughout downswing.

### E07: [06:47–08:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p_HZJ2u0TIo&t=407s)

Anecdote about Nicklaus foot-rolling practice introduces rolling inward in transition/down rather than immediately pushing the trail hip toward ball. Harrington wants right glute/pelvis moving under left. A high trail heel can coexist with this if knee moves across rather than toward ball; Justin Thomas is mentioned, but the visible example is Harrington's reenactment.

Limit: Historical claims not independently checked. Heel height alone cannot classify good or bad movement. Foot-roll instruction is downswing practice; earlier planted-foot cue refers to backswing.

### E08: [08:10–10:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p_HZJ2u0TIo&t=490s)

Right-under-left pelvis action is linked to closing the left hip crease, feeling left glute engaged and allowing left glute to move back. He demonstrates from front, side and rear, distinguishing muscular sensation from visible positions.

Limit: No measured muscle activation or pressure distribution. Feeling a glute working does not prove a particular force or diagnosis.

### E09: [10:00–11:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p_HZJ2u0TIo&t=600s)

Harrington warns against independently straightening lead knee merely to copy a powerful player's impact image. He demonstrates body extension upward while maintaining connected lead-side tension as the source of observed lead-leg straightening. Slow rehearsal can still show flex.

Limit: Body-up is a coaching feel here, not a measured ground-force intervention. Strong injury claims are not clinical findings. Do not require a locked knee at impact.

### E10: [11:44–13:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p_HZJ2u0TIo&t=704s)

He objects to hip separation when understood as hips spinning independently. Hips should lead and pull torso, not move rigidly together or disconnect. Cog and elastic-band analogies explain connected stretch across body into arms, which he relates to speed, face organization and shallowing.

Limit: This is a wording/feel distinction, not a denial that measurable hip-shoulder separation exists. No angular separation, torque, velocity or causal shallowing measurement.

### E11: [13:58–15:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p_HZJ2u0TIo&t=838s)

Staggered rehearsal draws the right foot back and moves the right knee/glute under the left side, closing lead hip crease. Foot rolls inward in practice; a heel lift may occur in full play once the desired direction is established. Suggested as a simple rehearsal that can be done without shots.

Limit: No exact foot offset or repetition dosage. Deliberate drill shape is not the normal full-swing template.

### E12: [15:18–16:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p_HZJ2u0TIo&t=918s)

## Applicability and measurement limits

The source has no TrackMan, force-plate, 3D, measured hip range or quantified outcome. Pressure and muscle engagement are instructor sensations. Pelvis angles, powerful-player examples and a two-yard throwing target must not become diagnostic thresholds.

The hip-crease pinch is a tactile cue, not pain. Relaxed setup does not mean locked knees. Hips pulling the torso does not mean rigidly moving both together, and the early transition back-to-target cue does not mean leaving the trunk behind throughout downswing.

Select one relevant rehearsal and verify transfer. Staggered stance and throwing are alternatives, not extra simultaneous instructions.
