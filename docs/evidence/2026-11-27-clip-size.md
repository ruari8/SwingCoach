# Auto clip file size

Issue: [#33](https://github.com/ruari8/SwingCoach/issues/33). Read-only spike; no app code changed.

Status: measured. A quarter of every Auto clip is frames that never play. A shorter keyframe interval in the rolling writer removes most of them. Nothing changed yet; that needs Ruari's sign-off and a phone run.

## Answer

A 240 fps Auto clip averages 23.5 MB. About 18 MB is the swing you see: 2.9 real seconds at the rolling writer's 50 Mbps. The other 5.9 MB on average (up to 11.9 MB) is footage before the clip starts that is stored in the file but never shown.

The cause is the export. It copies the rolling buffer's encoded frames instead of re-encoding them. A copied clip has to start on a keyframe, and the writer puts one only every 2 real seconds (`AVVideoMaxKeyFrameIntervalKey: expectedFPS * 2` in `AutoRollingVideoBuffer.makeWriter`). The file therefore starts at the keyframe before the clip, and an edit list hides the extra frames. Players and AVFoundation skip them; they still take up space.

## Measurements

The 23 Oct range session: 48 Auto clips from an iPhone 17 Pro (iPhone18,3, iOS 27.0.1), `quiet-rollover-v1`, Extra swing footage at 0 s. Clips are in `~/Downloads/23:10rangeSession` (local only).

| Clips | Capture | Writer bitrate | Saved clip | Frames shown | File size (mean) | Hidden frames (mean) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 37 | 1080p 240 fps, H.264 | 50 Mbps | 23.2 s at 30 fps (8× slow) | 696 | 23.5 MB | 5.9 MB, up to 239 frames |
| 11 | 1080p 120 fps, H.264 | 32 Mbps | 11.6 s at 30 fps (4× slow) | 348 | 15.5 MB | 4.2 MB, up to 115 frames |
| 48 | all | | | | 21.7 MB (11.7–29.6) | 5.5 MB, 25% of all bytes |

Each clip has 2 or 3 keyframes. There's no audio track; Auto never records sound.

The session took 1.04 GB, of which 0.26 GB was hidden frames. Save to Photos is on by default, so each swing is stored twice on the phone: in the app (excluded from backup) and in Photos.

The 6 Sep session (`~/Downloads/6:9_range_session`) shows the same pattern from an older build. Its Auto clips are 19.2 s, 15–29 MB, H.264 with no audio. Up to 197 frames are hidden.

The other files in that folder:

- **Manual clips** (`swing_*.MP4`, about 31 MB): H.264 at 12.6 Mbps plus a 228 kbps AAC sound track. They are re-encoded, so nothing is hidden. Manual capture is now off by default.
- **`IMG_4627.mov`** (2 GB): a 23-minute iPhone Camera recording, 1080p HEVC at about 11.7 Mbps. It isn't an app clip.

## Options

Frame rate and resolution stay as they are in every option below. Pose, the club detector and the P positions need 240 fps at 1080p.

| Option | Saving per 240 fps clip | Quality | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Keyframe every 0.25 real seconds** (`expectedFPS / 4`) | About 4.7 MB (22%). Hidden frames fall from about 1 s on average to about 0.13 s | Slightly lower per frame at the same average bitrate, because there are more keyframes | Encoder load during Auto, a sensitive area. Needs a phone cadence run |
| **2. HEVC in the rolling writer** | About 30–40% of the visible part at equal quality (Mac test below) | Same | Encoder load at 240 fps alongside the detector; reading HEVC in Mac tools. Needs a phone cadence run |
| **3. Re-encode to HEVC when the clip is saved** | Hidden frames gone, plus HEVC savings | A second encode, so a small loss | Adds encode time between the swing and the Library. Doesn't touch Auto capture |
| 4. Lower the 240 fps bitrate (for example 50 → 30 Mbps) | About 40% | Mac test: PSNR about 4 dB lower. Could blur the club further under floodlights, which already costs detections ([Nov 6](./2026-11-06-range-session-missed-swings.md)) | Not recommended |

### HEVC compared with H.264

Two clips were re-encoded on the Mac with Apple's hardware encoders (`h264_videotoolbox`, `hevc_videotoolbox`) and compared with the original. The reference is already compressed, so treat these as relative numbers.

| Encoder, bitrate on the 30 fps timeline | Size | SSIM | PSNR (dB) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H.264, 50 Mbps | 139 MB | 0.9988 | 54.5–54.9 |
| H.264, 30 Mbps | 83 MB | 0.9971 | 50.4–50.5 |
| HEVC, 30 Mbps | 89 MB | 0.9978 | 52.4–52.7 |
| HEVC, 20 Mbps | 60 MB | 0.9960 | 49.6–49.7 |
| HEVC, 15 Mbps | 45 MB | 0.9943 | 47.8 |

At the same bitrate, HEVC is about 2 dB better. HEVC at about two thirds of the H.264 bitrate roughly matches H.264.

## Recommendation

1. Do option 1 first. It's a one-line change that removes most of the wasted quarter without touching quality settings. Check it with a phone footage run for cadence and dropped frames, then re-measure hidden bytes with the command below.
2. If storage still matters after that, try option 3 before option 2, because it keeps the encoder work out of Auto capture. Time the extra encode on the phone first.
3. Leave the bitrate, frame rate and resolution as they are.

Separately, check that the Mac tools that read clips with OpenCV (`detector_workbench/validation/propose_p7_labels.py`, `detector_workbench/modeling/build_swing_frame_dataset.py`) honour the edit list. If they don't, their frame 0 is a hidden frame and not the clip's first shown frame. ffmpeg and AVFoundation honour it.

## Reproduce

```bash
# Hidden frames and bytes in one clip (packets with negative timestamps)
ffprobe -v error -select_streams v -show_entries packet=pts_time,size,flags -of csv clip.mp4
```
